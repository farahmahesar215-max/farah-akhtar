Teaching Philosophy

I teach so that students leave able to use an idea, not only recall it. In economics and management that means starting from a situation students recognise, such as a small business deciding whether to go digital or a household reacting to rising prices, and then building the concept and the tools around it.

My first teaching was a management course for computer science students. Many of them did not see why management mattered to them, so I tied each topic to the projects and teams they would work in. That habit has stayed with me: I ask who is in the room and connect the material to where they are going.

My research uses survey data and structural equation modelling, and I bring that into class. Students work with real datasets, read published studies critically and learn to ask what the numbers can and cannot show. I want graduates who can question a claim, check the evidence and explain it clearly.

I treat feedback as a two-way process. I check understanding often, adjust when a topic is not landing, and take learner evaluations seriously. Having coordinated semester-wise teacher evaluations as an academic officer, I know how much they reveal, and I use them to improve the next run of a course.

Research-Led Teaching & Student Support

  • Real data in class. My PhD on digital transformation, ESG and SME financial performance gives me survey datasets and cases from Malaysian firms that students can analyse directly.
  • Methods support. I can guide students and colleagues through PLS-SEM and CB-SEM in SmartPLS 4, AMOS and R, from questionnaire design to reporting results.
  • Publishing literacy. As a peer reviewer for Scopus and Springer Nature journals and a published author, I can show students how research is judged and how to choose credible journals.
  • Award-winning research communication. Best Paper Award at BAFE 2024, plus presentations at BAFE 2023 and the UTAR–UMSIDA Forum.
