Teaching Philosophy

I teach so that students leave able to use an idea, not only recall it. In economics and management that means starting from a situation students recognise, such as a small business deciding whether to go digital or a household reacting to rising prices, and then building the concept and the tools around it.

Some of my most useful teaching was Management Principles for computer science students. Many of them did not see why management mattered to them, so I used applied cases and group work tied to the projects and teams they would work in. That habit has stayed with me: I ask who is in the room and connect the material to where they are going.

My research uses survey data and structural equation modelling, and I bring that into class. Students work with real datasets, read published studies critically and learn to ask what the numbers can and cannot show. I want graduates who can question a claim, check the evidence and explain it clearly.

I treat feedback as a two-way process. I give students individual feedback on their work, check understanding often and adjust when a topic is not landing. Having run campus-wide teacher evaluations as an academic officer, I know how much they reveal, and I use them to improve the next run of a course.

  • Real data in class. My PhD on digital transformation, ESG and SME financial performance gives me survey datasets and cases from Malaysian firms that students can analyse directly.
  • Methods support. I can guide students and colleagues through survey design and PLS-SEM or CB-SEM in SmartPLS 4, AMOS and R, from questionnaire development to reporting results.
  • Publishing literacy. As a peer reviewer for six Scopus and Web of Science indexed journals and a published author, I can show students how research is judged and how to choose credible journals.
